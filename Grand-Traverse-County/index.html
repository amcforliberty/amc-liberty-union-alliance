TRAVERSE CITY RECORD-EAGLE — OCTOBER 9, 2026

Flock Cameras Land on GT Board's Radar

Published Investigative Feature | Print Edition Page 5A | Reporting by Aly Kleidon

Following organized civic deployments, independent constitutional advocates and concerned regional residents entered the Grand Traverse County Board session to challenge un-voted automated license plate reader procurement networks.

"Williamsburg resident Augustus Chell provided a public comment on the county's Flock cameras... 'Private technology vendors bypass this commission's centralized legislative oversight by executing fragmented township contracts to rig Traverse City with an intrusive, auto-surveillance wall... These independent township cameras do not operate in a vacuum; they rely on an un-voted backdoor subsidy from this county...'"
"Political candidates Dr. Mitch Distin and Kate Gallup were among those who spoke out against the cameras, citing similar use of Flock cameras taking place with the Antrim County Board of Commissioners."

Multi-County Coalition Record:

The Alliance records and acknowledges the parallel statutory tracking work being executed across regional lines by our coalition partners:

  • Dr. Mitchell Distin (Candidate for Michigan House District 104): Spearheaded a packed-house turnout at the October 1st Antrim meeting, deploying six comprehensive statutory record scrolls for citizens to read directly into the public record. Distin has actively coordinated multi-county FOIA tracking metrics, declaring: "We are not anti-police, we are anti-surveillance... if folks can't tell the difference, it's because they haven't been watching what's going on."
  • Kate Gallup (Candidate for Michigan Senate District 37): Standing in solidarity with residents at both the packed Antrim assembly and the Grand Traverse board sessions to audit procurement channels and prevent corporate technology contracts from overriding local citizen protections.
